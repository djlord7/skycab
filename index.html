    How the curves work

    1. Flutes top to bottom. Line up the brown FLUTES arrow with the ridges inside the cardboard. Bent that way, cardboard curves smoothly instead of kinking.
    2. Pre-roll. Before gluing, roll each wall piece over a table edge or a rolling pin, printed side out, until it holds a gentle curve by itself.
    3. Green press lines run across every wall piece, 2 cm apart, along the lines it has to bend on. The pieces fan out, so towards one end the bend runs up to 25° across the flutes and would kink without them. On the inside face, run a blunt butter knife or an empty ballpoint firmly along each one. Press, don't cut. Then bend a little at every line, like closing a fan: the wall goes round in small even steps.
    4. Glue tabs. Every wall edge has a row of tabs with V gaps between them. Fold them inward along the blue dashed line; the gaps let them follow the curve. Hot-glue them under the deck and to the belt and bottom rings.
    5. Tape while it sets. Hold each joint with masking tape on the outside. The paper skin later hides the small facets from the press lines.
    6. Strips: rotor rim R3 and seat strip R8. Cut them so the waves run ACROSS the strip; the cutting layouts already do this. Roll the strip round a 2-litre bottle, smooth side out, moving along a little at a time. Then let it spring open: it will sit on the 22 cm rotor disc (R3) or round the 6 cm seat corners (R8) without a crease. Hold it with clothes pegs or a rubber band while the glue sets.
    7. Still fighting you? Mist the inside face lightly with a spray bottle (damp, not wet), roll it round the bottle again, peg it and let it dry: it keeps the curve. For anything tighter than a jam jar, peel the paper off the inside face so the ridges show; it then bends like a hinge, and the smooth outside stays neat.

    Ruler cuts

    Straight strips and tube lengths, measured with a steel ruler. Every cut on the tubes themselves has a printed paper guide: O for the strut ends, P for the pods.

    Tubes that don't go floppy

    1. Best: cardboard mailing tubes. It's recyclable and stiff. The ones bought are 7.0 cm across (22 cm round the outside) and 59 cm long. The strut R1 must be one unbroken piece, so one tube gives the strut, and a second tube gives both pods R2: you need 2, or 3 with a spare. Measure the diameter and type it into "Tube diameter" above so guides O and P fit it exactly.
    2. Also good: aluminium-foil or cling-film roll cores. They're much thicker than kitchen-roll tubes. Join two end to end with a 6 cm sleeve cut from a third, glued inside.
    3. Make your own: roll 3 layers of cereal-box card round a bottle of the right size, with PVA glue brushed between the layers. Leave it to dry overnight on the bottle. Rolled corrugated cardboard is too soft for the strut.
    4. Stiffer still: push a 1 cm wooden dowel or two bamboo skewers through the strut beside the wires and glue the ends. Then the rotors can't droop.

    Which glue for what

    1. Hot glue gun, normal temperature (40-60 W, 11 mm sticks): for every tab and every tube joint. It soaks into the paper and grips in about 10 seconds. Low-temperature guns grip cardboard noticeably less.
    2. Fevicol MR (white glue): for anything glued flat, face to face. Strongest once fully dry, wipes off while wet. Spread it thin with a scrap of card; a thick coat makes the card wavy.
    3. Both together where you want the most strength: Fevicol over most of the tab and a few dots of hot glue to hold it straight away.
    4. Skip Fevikwik (goes brittle on cardboard) and stringy rubber-based glues.

    What makes it strong

    1. Glue the whole tab, not a dot in the middle, and press each one for a full 10 seconds.
    2. Leave Fevicol parts overnight before cutting slots or bending them.
    3. Finish with a hot-glue bead along the inside seams where the walls meet the deck and the rings.
    4. Test before painting: wear it, shake it gently, tug the rotors. Anything that creaks gets another bead.
    5. Hot glue burns, so an adult should do the gluing; keep a bowl of cold water nearby.

    Motors and wiring

    Battery box hidden under the deck behind her; its switch is your master switch and turns the headlights on. Wires are taped under the right-hand deck edge: one pair through the pilot's TAKE-OFF button to both motors, one pair on to the headlights. No fancy channels needed.

    You needWhere it goes
    Finger safety. These small motors spin at several thousand rpm. Use soft card blades, never hard plastic. Close each rotor with a clear disc on top and underneath, and fit the battery box where only you can reach the switch. It runs on 3 × AA (4.5 V), which the motors are rated for. Use AA batteries, never coin cells.

    Build order

    1. Print and check scale. Print at 100% (Actual size). Measure the 10 × 10 cm square on page 1 both ways before cutting anything.
    2. Paper fitting. Tape the deck tiles (A), cut out the waist hole and slip it over her like a skirt. If it pinches, change her sizes here and ask for new stencils.
    3. Trace onto cardboard. Match each FLUTES arrow to the ridges. Half patterns: trace, flip on the red line, trace again.
    4. Deck and beams. Cut the deck, waist hole and strap slots. Glue beams D1 and D2 under the deck on the dotted lines.
    5. Upper walls. Pre-roll each piece, press the green lines, fold the edge tabs in. Join C1 + C2 for each side with joiner strips. Glue the top edge to the deck edge from the nose backwards, then glue the belt ring B1 inside the bottom edge.
    6. Lower walls. Pre-roll, press the green lines, fold the tabs in. Join C3 + C4 for each side, glue them to the belt ring, then glue bottom ring B2 inside the bottom edge. The crease at the belt is where the checker stripe goes.
    7. Strut and pods. Follow "Fitting the tubes": cut the 2 mailing tubes, shape the pods with template P, cut a fish-mouth on both strut ends with guide O, push the strut through both wall holes, glue it to beam D1, then glue a pod upright on each end.
    8. Motor wires. Solder leads to both motors first, and run the wires through the strut and up into each pod before the pods are glued on (tube step 5). The motors themselves go in with the rotors, next.
    9. Rotors. Wrap each motor in collar R9 until it just fits the socket in the bottom layer of base disc Q, push it in until the motor face touches the top layer, shaft up through the centre hole, and hot-glue round the collar. Spin the shaft by hand to check it stands square. Wrap collar Y round the top of the pod tube, curved edge flush with the slanted top, and fold its tabs out flat. Glue Q onto the tube top and the collar tabs, on its oval mark, with the motor hanging inside the pod and the wires running down it. Run a bead of hot glue round the outside of the join under Q. Wrap rim R3 round Q with its bottom tabs glued under Q and its top tabs folded in, then glue lip ring I onto the top tabs. Press the bottle-cap hub (open side up) with four card blades onto the shaft, then tape a clear disc on top.
    10. Seat, tail, sign. Glue the front seat panel E to the deck (its tab folds into the box). Glue one row of R8's tabs to the inside of it round the sides and top. Run glue along R8's other row of tabs and the back panel's deck tab, and press the back panel on. Cut the back, top and front edge tabs off the flipped tail fin side K. Bend both sides on the green lines, glue their base tabs to the deck and up the back of the seat, then glue the remaining edge tabs inside the other side. Fold the TAXI box L and glue its bottom tabs to the top of the seat.
    11. Battery, button, wiring. Velcro the battery box under the deck behind her. Push the 16 mm button through its hole on the right-hand deck rim. Wire: battery, through the hole in D2, taped under the right deck edge to the button, then into the strut tube. Tape the headlight wire alongside it to the nose. Test the motors now, before painting.
    12. Face and tail lights. Paint the nose panel black. Headlights: glue white lens V behind each egg-shaped window in C1. Make the light box: cut out strip W with its V notches, paint the middle white, glue its top tabs to the inside of the wall round the window (over the lens), push a 10 mm LED (resistor on its long leg) through the centre of back X from behind, hot-glue it, and glue X onto W's back tabs. On the outside, glue trim U round the window and hood R7 over it like an eyelid. Wire both straight to the battery. Paint the rear panel black and glue the red tail lights M at the corners.
    13. Windshield, dashboard, straps. Glue dashboard G in place, then cut windshield S from clear plastic, fold its tabs out and tape them along line S on the deck. Black electrical tape on the top edge. Thread the straps and do a fit test: walk, turn, climb a step, salute.
    14. Skin and paint (no full papier-mâché). Masking tape over every seam and cut edge. Then one layer of plain paper (newspaper or brown kraft) brushed on with PVA glue mixed 2 parts glue to 1 part water, over the body, seat and fin only. Let it dry overnight, give it one coat of white primer, then paint. Mask the rotors and windshield first. Like the blueprint: a checker stripe of two rows of 2 cm squares just below the belt crease, from the black nose back to just before the tail lights. Paint a 4 cm white band, then stick on 2 cm squares of black electrical tape like a chessboard. Paint a big black "01" (about 4 cm tall) on each upper side wall, between the headlight and the strut. Add a dark grey strip along the bottom edge.

    Getting it accurate

    • Turn off "Fit to page" when printing. Most printers shrink pages by 3–6% by default, enough to make the walls miss the deck.
    • For the most accurate result, print the A0 file at a print or plotter shop. No taping.
    • Cut the paper templates exactly on the line, then trace with a sharp pencil held upright.
    • Use a fresh blade and a steel ruler, and make several light passes instead of one hard one.
    • Dry-fit each wall with masking tape before gluing. The four quarters should meet at the nose, both sides and the rear.
    • Run the flutes of the two seat panels in different directions (one up and down, one across). The seat box is then very stiff.
    • Check the balance (red dot on the model). If the nose dips when she wears it, tape a spare AA pair under the deck next to the battery box.
